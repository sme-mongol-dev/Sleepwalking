# Visual module review

Date: 14 November 2026. Scope: `Assets/SleepwalkingVisual/` and `Docs/Integration/Visual.md`.

An independent reviewer checked the source against `MVP.md`, `ROOM_SCENE_BRIEF.md`, `UI_BRIEF.md`, and the four extracted room frames. The review found an unsafe preview-builder scene replacement, an unused pixel-width setting, a wake-count initialization risk, and weak correspondence to the room reference. These were corrected: preview generation now uses an additive temporary scene; both pixel dimensions govern the render texture; wake changes capture the initial state; the room now includes the reference's wide bed, high shelf, central clock and calendar, right window, and nightstand. The required Quit door is placed at the right edge.

Integration source review found matching prefab paths and API calls in Game Mechanics and UI. Game Mechanics uses `SetWakeCount`, loads the four theme prefabs, and applies `DreamTheme` to level geometry. UI recognises hotspot actions and labels and scales pointer coordinates for the camera render texture. The reviewer reported a separate Game Mechanics fallback raycast with unscaled pointer coordinates to the orchestrator.

The direct Unity 6000.3.28f1 build compiled C# and generated the room prefab, eleven palette materials, and all four theme prefabs. A separate reviewer inspected their YAML: the room contains 116 editable objects, 104 renderers, five hotspot colliders paired with the required actions, and the expected camera and wake-change references. Each theme has its own identifier, 23 objects and palette material references. The reviewer also verified that the integrated `Sleepwalking.unity` scene references the generated room and all four theme GUIDs in the intended order.

The first isolated `RoomPreview.unity` was empty. After correcting preview creation and moving Unity logs out of `Assets`, the final direct Editor run regenerated it. Independent recheck confirmed exactly one prefab instance linked to the room prefab and one scene root. `Docs/QA/VisualLogs/VisualPreviewRepair.log` records successful batch exit with no C# compiler, null-reference or asset-import errors. Rendered appearance and runtime hover remain unverified because no graphical play capture was performed in this review.

## Menu visual polish — 14 November

Acceptance criteria for the new render: at both 16:9 and 4:3, the sleeping child and bed must read as the focal subject; the bookshelf, clock, door and window must remain distinct at the pixel output resolution; the composition must retain the reference's horizontal bed, wall shelf, clock, calendar and moonlit right window; visible layered surfaces must give the bed and room depth. All authored material colours stay within the UI brief palette, and the room remains editable as separate 3D objects with the same hotspot actions.

The first Player captures (`player-screen-after-ui.png`, `player-screen-after-ui-4x3.png`) failed the visual criterion: the bed and child looked flat, the blue curtain dominated, and the wall had little depth. The independent Art lead called out those issues before the polish pass.

`VisualPolishBuildEscalated.log` records the successful direct Unity Editor rebuild of `SleepwalkingRoom.prefab`, `RoomPreview.unity` and the four dream-theme prefabs. The room now has a raised front rail, bedposts, layered mattress/pillow/quilt with folds, a complete sleeping child with cap and hands, recessed night window with dark curtains, wall shadow panels, a lamp, a deeper door and a darker calendar. The low-resolution `PixelRoomView`, five hotspot colliders and room-change API remain. A new integrated Player capture and Art lead verdict are pending the coordinated Windows rebuild after the UI changes.

The coordinated Player captures `player-screen-polish-16x9.png` and `player-screen-polish-4x3.png` exposed a visual regression. The independent Art lead ranked the partially cropped Quit door in 4:3 as P0; the solid brown window, nearly blank calendar, black quilt mass and poorly separated hero/pillow as P1. The UI title overlapping upper objects was recorded as a separate UI composition issue. In Visual code, the window layers now have explicit front-to-back depth, the calendar uses contrasting grid lines, the blanket's front drape is thin and its upper surface is brighter, the pillow and skin differ, the pajamas have a distinct colour, and the large left wall rectangle is gone. `PixelRoomView.MinimumHalfWorldWidth` on the room camera keeps all menu hotspots within the 4:3 view while leaving gameplay cameras unchanged by default. `VisualPolishCorrection.log` records a successful prefab/preview regeneration with Unity exit code 0. These corrections still require a new Player capture before visual acceptance.

The Art lead compared the next actual Player images, `player-screen-correction-16x9.png` and `player-screen-correction-4x3.png`, against the earlier captures and room video. The window again shows the dark sky and moon; the boy, pajamas, bed and shelf are legible at both aspect ratios. The right-side door is within the 4:3 frame, resolving the earlier visibility blocker. Remaining P1 defects in those captures: the calendar reads as vertical bars, the quilt has one long light line, and the child still has a schematic face. The lead calls this a usable dark pixel-art prototype for choosing variants, not a final-quality room. The title overlap is a UI composition issue outside this module.

The final Visual pass moved the calendar rows forward and thickened them, broke the quilt highlight into short folds, darkened the mattress top to distinguish it from skin, and strengthened the door latch/knob. Because Game Mechanics reuses the room camera in dream levels, `PixelRoomView` now applies its minimum world width only while the linked `RoomEnvironment` is active and the camera is at its original room position. `VisualFinalCorrection.log` records successful Unity prefab generation (exit code 0), and the prefab serializes `RoomRoot` alongside `MinimumHalfWorldWidth: 7.8`. This last pass has **not** been assessed in a Windows Player frame; a final coordinated build and 16:9/4:3 capture are required to verify calendar, quilt, door and dream camera framing.

The independent Art lead rechecked the final prefab structure: the `RoomRoot` reference, moved calendar rows, three short quilt folds, secondary mattress material and brighter door details are serialized. The lead keeps visual acceptance open until the next Player capture. Dream themes were left unchanged while Level Design prepares separate navigation accents outside the recoloured geometry.
