# Level Design → Game Mechanics

Seven authored prefabs live in `Assets/SleepwalkingLevels/Prefabs/`. `L01` through `L07` are ordered dream geometries. Instantiate one at a time as the active level root at `(0,0,0)`; do not rescale it. Geometry coordinates are in Unity world units, with gameplay on XY and depth on Z. The root has `SleepwalkingLevelDefinition` with a stable `LevelIndex`, editable `FirstCycleStyle` (a placeholder for the player's later choice), and references to its start and exit markers. Repeat these same prefabs on every cycle; change only style and runtime state.

The `Geometry`, `Hazards`, `Mechanisms`, `Markers`, and `Readability` child groups keep each item independently editable. Every solid platform and wall in `Geometry`, plus each timed or moving platform in `Mechanisms`, has `Sleepwalking.Levels.SleepwalkingTileSurface`. Its `Solid` property returns the same GameObject's `BoxCollider2D`; the collider and Transform define the tile-covered XY rectangle. The tile renderer must not change that rectangle or its physics. Gameplay solids and traps carry `Collider2D` on their main GameObject; their matching 3D collider is on a `Collision3D` child, since Unity forbids both physics collider families on one GameObject. Do not enable both physics systems on the player. Hazard previews have trigger colliders; Game Mechanics should attach its damage component to each `SleepwalkingLevelFeature` with kind `Spikes`, `Saw`, `LaserBase`, or `LaserBeam`. Laser beams preview at 0.2 world units (3.6 rendered pixels at 320×180, orthographic size 5). At runtime, read `BeamPixelWidth` (3.5) and set both visual and hitbox width to that many rendered pixels under the active pixel render resolution and camera size. The triangular base is independently lethal. `BedExit` has a trigger and `Exit` marker; touching it runs the sleep/room transition. `PlayerStart` is a transform marker and has no collider.

The `FallReset` feature is a trigger strip below the full route at Y=-3.2. Connect it to the same retry animation and camera reset as trap contact so a missed jump cannot leave the player falling forever. L04 places only its visible bed parts 0.2 world units lower to sit on its continuous floor; the `BedExit`, `Frame`, and `Pillow` collider objects retain their original positions.

Each `SleepwalkingLevelFeature` has an editable `FeatureId`, `Kind`, `LinkedFeatureId`, `ActiveSeconds`, `MoveOffset`, `CycleSeconds`, and `PhaseSeconds`. A button's `LinkedFeatureId` names the hazard or group to disable while its timer runs. `L04_ButtonLaser` targets `L04_Laser`; `L07_ButtonA` targets `L07_Laser`; `L07_ButtonB` targets `L07_Saw`. Button timers are set in the prefab. Timed platforms are solid for `ActiveSeconds` (2.8) in each `CycleSeconds` (4) cycle, offset by `PhaseSeconds` (0/0.3/0); moving platforms travel between their authored position and `MoveOffset` and back over `CycleSeconds`. The current SleepwalkingMechanism connects the timed and moving 2D colliders at runtime. Mechanisms should restore initial positions and states on each retry and clear paint on exit, while preserving geometry.

The authored route assumes a player collision width <= 0.65, height <= 1.2, repeatable jump height >= 1.4, running leap >= 2.4, wall grab/jump on vertical surfaces, and dash travel >= 2.8. Gaps are <= 1.8 unless a dash or moving platform is explicitly introduced. Starting feet should be placed at the top of the start block. Coordinate-specific routes and expected abilities are in `Assets/SleepwalkingLevels/README.md`. If the final controller falls short of these values, tune the controller or the corresponding prefab's individual blocks; do not change the cycle's geometry at runtime.

The level package does not edit the shared assembly scene, gameplay scripts, or style assets. The editor generator `Sleepwalking/Levels/Build Seven Prefabs` can rebuild the prefab assets from source layouts if needed, but manual prefab changes should be kept and not overwritten casually.

Each dream has two visual layers. The background is a separate, collider-free theme layer; temporary generated backgrounds are acceptable. The foreground gameplay layer uses the original `Docs/UI/References/TL_Meat_Dream.png`, `TL_PostApoc_Dream.png`, `TL_DeepWater_Dream.png`, or `TL_Heaven_Dream.png` as tile atlases for the solid surfaces marked above. These four 96×192 PNGs are production tile sources, not moodboards. Visual Director imports them as point-filtered 32×32 sprites and selects the matching atlas through `DreamTheme.Id`. Tile renderers should be children of their surface, so moving/timed platforms carry and hide their tiles with the physics object. Static `Geometry` blocks may extend their tile art downward beyond the collider to form a visible top, side, and lower corner; this visual skirt has no collider and must leave the horizontal gaps clear. Apply tile art after `DreamTheme.ApplyTo` or exclude it from that palette substitution. The legacy solid `SafeFace` overlays were removed to expose the tiles. `Readability` retains collider-free pale top edges, now 0.04 world units tall (about one pixel at 320×240 with orthographic size 5), and a wall-grip stripe. These should stay visible without covering the atlas. Hazard meshes use `#E04020`; buttons use `#229EDE`; timed platforms have three cyan tabs, while moving platforms have a continuous cyan edge and two pale direction marks. Bed parts use a dark silhouette with a pale frame, headboard, footboard, blanket, and pillow. `FallReset` has no renderer. `SleepwalkingLevelVisualState` follows the 2D collider's enabled state and hides a disabled hazard or timed platform plus its child tile renderers and cues.

## Current integration checks for Game Mechanics

The current `SleepwalkingMechanism` reads the feature metadata through `ConnectAuthoredLevel`; the level-owned `SleepwalkingLevelVisualState` handles child mesh visibility when its 2D collider changes. Timed platforms now use the prefab's `ActiveSeconds` (2.8 of 4 seconds). Moving platforms start at their authored position, move through a kinematic `Rigidbody2D`, and pass their velocity to a standing player. `SleepwalkingGame.BeamWidth()` uses `GameCamera.targetTexture.height` when a RenderTexture is active; its fallback also accounts for the current aspect ratio. `UpdateBeamWidths()` runs during the dream and rescales laser beams when the render height changes. This is intended to keep a 3.5-pixel beam at both 320×180 and 320×240. Confirm visible beam and trigger width in Play mode at both sizes before accepting the integration.

L07 extends to X=21.1 to require a dash under the current controller. Visual Director's backdrop now spans X −12 to 32 and Y −5.2 to 8.8 so the far exit has background coverage; this backdrop remains separate from the tiled gameplay surface and has no colliders.

## Traversal calibration — design inventory

The contact-based rerun in `Docs/QA/movement-trajectory-results.xml` confirmed bypasses in the previous flat L05/L06 geometry. The calibrated prefabs were rebuilt afterward; the old XML does not validate the new layout. L07 jump+dash landed on its far support for Controlled/Responsive in an isolated test with disabled hazards, and a later full-route test with both buttons active passed for those two profiles.

The authored Player uses a 0.55×1.0 `BoxCollider2D`; its horizontal half-width is 0.275. The intervals below are target **player-centre** landing windows, not trigger extents. They keep 0.15 world units of extra inset on a static block and 0.10 on a narrow timed/moving pad, beyond full collider support. The listed minimum horizontal advance starts at the last fully supported centre on the departure surface, without coyote time. The selected continuous PlayMode routes and measured landing contacts are in `Docs/QA/traversal-calibration-results.md`; the old free-flight estimates in the QA report do not establish that a move succeeds.

Static audit of the previous geometry explained the direct L05/L06 bypasses. The rebuilt L05 staircase makes each neighbouring rise 1.3 while a skipped platform requires a rise of 2.6, above the candidate plain-jump apexes (2.15–2.29). The rebuilt L06 places LiftLanding 3.25 above Start and 1.95 above the initial horizontal mover, with a 10.00 centre advance from that mover to the safe landing window; HighExit is 2.70 above LiftLanding. These are design constraints pending PlayMode tests, including airborne repeated dash and wall-jump against one-way supports.
