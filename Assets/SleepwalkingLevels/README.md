# Dream and tutorial geometries

Use `Sleepwalking/Levels/Build Tutorial Prefabs` after changing `Editor/SleepwalkingTutorialBuilder.cs` or the world-space signs. It builds `Tutorial01_DreamGeometry.prefab` and `Tutorial02_DreamGeometry.prefab` separately from L01–L07. Both have the same 32-unit root scale, solid tile matrix and collider-free theme background as the dreams. Their `LevelIndex` values are -2 and -1; the game keeps them outside the repeating seven-dream cycle.

Tutorial01 has a continuous start floor `[0,11)` at top 0, a hanging grip wall `[7,8)` from Y=2 to 8, and an exit ledge `[11,18)` at top 3. The world signs teach A/D, Space, grip and wall jump; dash is disabled by Game Mechanics for this stage. Tutorial02 has a start floor `[0,7)`, a six-cell gap with 12 visible spikes on its solid foundation, and a final floor `[13,20)`. The world sign teaches Shift; the gap is designed to require dash. `Readability/TutorialHints` holds sign prefab instances so they can be edited without rebuilding the level geometry.

The tutorials reuse the theme-neutral `SleepwalkingTileSurface` blocks and the existing bed trigger. `DreamTheme` supplies the selected atlas, background and bed art at runtime. The initial `Meat` style in each definition is the same placeholder used by L01–L07; it does not embed a theme texture into the geometry.

## Seven dream geometries

Use `Sleepwalking/Levels/Build Seven Prefabs` after changing the authored layouts. Place any `Prefabs/L##_DreamGeometry.prefab` under the shared scene's level root at `(0,0,0)` without rescaling it: the prefab itself carries the 32-unit cell scale. The scene builder should use the root `SleepwalkingLevelDefinition` and connect behaviors through `SleepwalkingLevelFeature`; see `Docs/Integration/Levels.md`. Every block, hazard, button, and bed is an independent prefab child. Keep `Editor/SleepwalkingLevelBuilder.cs`, `Tools/generate-prefabs.cjs`, and the seven prefab assets synchronized when changing layout.

The first cycle style is an editable enum on each prefab. All seven currently carry the `Meat` placeholder because the player's exact distribution is pending. The geometry is style neutral; use the selected style only for material, ambience, and dressing overrides at runtime. Keep the same prefab for later cycles.

## Tile foreground and background

Every solid platform and wall has a `SleepwalkingTileSurface` marker. Its `Solid` property returns its `Collider2D`; the saved solid tile surfaces use `PolygonCollider2D`. The prefab root scales XY by 32: local integer cell coordinates become 32 Unity units per cell, with world origin `(0,0)`. `Geometry/CaveShell` fills the area outside each route. Static platforms and shell blocks form one occupied-cell matrix, with a tile chosen from north/east/south/west neighbours. Atlas cells 1–16 are solid cardinal-neighbour variants. Cell 17 is a solid inner-corner variant when all four cardinal neighbours and exactly three diagonals are occupied; its south-east painted corner rotates toward the one empty diagonal. Cell 18 is the double inner-corner variant for exactly two adjacent empty diagonals; its south-west and south-east painted corners rotate toward those openings. Moving and timed platforms start on the same grid but remain separate visual islands so they can move and disappear. The grid applies only to solid blocks; hazards and other objects need not align to cells. The background is a separate collider-free theme layer. Switching dream style does not change geometry or physics.

`SleepwalkingTileCollisionBuilder` shapes static solid polygons from the combined occupancy matrix: exposed outer corners are trimmed by up to four source pixels and tile-17 inner corners by two source pixels. Shared joins and the straight parts of top, bottom and side faces remain on the 32-unit grid. Timed and moving platforms retain rectangular polygons so their landing edges and motion contacts remain stable. All four dream styles share these physical contours; their different painted silhouettes can still extend beyond or fall inside the common contour. Buttons, hazards and bed triggers retain their authored colliders. The Unity builders shape both tutorials and L01–L07; the JS generator shapes L01–L07. The `Sleepwalking/Levels/Shape Solid Tile Colliders` command repairs previously saved prefabs that still contain solid tile boxes.

The collider-free `Readability` group keeps movement cues; the wall-grip stripe, platform-wide edge strips and earlier solid dark faces were removed so they cannot cover the atlas. Readability marks do not extend the gameplay collision. Hazards are orange, buttons cyan, timed platforms have three cyan tabs, and moving platforms have a cyan edge with pale direction marks. The bed has a dark silhouette and pale frame. Visible spike rows cover every lethal open gap. `SleepwalkingLevelVisualState` hides a disabled hazard or timed platform and child tile renderers/cues when its 2D collider is disabled.

Each `BedExit` sits on the final platform of its route. The bed visuals and trigger use the same authored height.

Gameplay `Collider2D` components remain on the authored objects. Matching 3D colliders live on their `Collision3D` children because Unity cannot put both physics collider families on one GameObject. The current 2D collider signatures for the two tutorials and seven dreams are recorded by `Tools/check-colliders.cjs` and `Tools/collider-baseline.json`.

The 14 PNGs in `Previews/` and earlier PlayMode captures show the previous fractional-coordinate geometry. They do not validate the 32-unit grid. Inspect fresh Unity Player frames after the new prefab build and tile renderer are connected.
